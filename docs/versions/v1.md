# v1 — single-outcome scorer (superseded)

**Status: historical.** No longer in the codebase or trained on disk —
superseded by [v2](v2.md). Documented here because the reasoning behind
replacing it is part of the project's record, not because it's something to
go run.

## What it was

The first working version of the learned scorer. Same feature vector, same
guesser pool, same overall three-stage pipeline (build similarity tensor →
generate training data → train → score at play time) as every version since
— the difference was entirely in what the model predicted and how that
prediction got turned into a decision.

- **Output**: a 5-way distribution over `k` alone — how many own-words a
  guesser reveals before *any* miss (`k` in 0..4, with `k=4` a right-censored
  "4 or more" bucket). Nothing about *why* a stop happened; a stop on
  neutral, opponent, or assassin were all the same label.
- **Scoring**: since the label couldn't distinguish the cause of a miss,
  every predicted miss was charged one flat, configurable `miss_penalty`
  (default -10, the assassin's own value) — a worst-case-flavored
  simplification where even a likely-neutral miss was scored as if it might
  have been the assassin.
- **Attempts rule**: went through its own internal revision during this
  version's lifetime — originally the standard Codenames `n+1`-attempts
  convention (announce `n`, get `n+1` guesses), later changed to exactly `n`
  attempts, no bonus guess (see `docs/log.md`'s numbering-convention
  entries). Both attempts rules existed only under the k-alone
  representation; the `n`-only rule is what v2 inherited and kept.
- **Noise tuning**: the guesser pool's `noise_std` was found to be
  ~2-3x the real measured similarity spread across a board (0.15 vs. an
  observed ~0.05-0.08), and was lowered to 0.03 as a result — this finding
  carried forward into v2 unchanged, it wasn't specific to the k-alone
  representation.

## Why it was replaced

The k-alone label meant the model could never learn that an
opponent-adjacent clue is meaningfully less dangerous than an
assassin-adjacent one — both just looked like "risk of an early stop" to
both the training signal and the scoring formula. That's a real correctness
gap, not a cosmetic one: it directly under-penalizes assassin risk relative
to opponent/neutral risk in aggregate (by charging all three the same),
and it removes any way to use the true, different reward values for each
category, or to tune risk aversion for the assassin specifically without
also (incorrectly) discounting opponent/neutral risk by the same amount.

v2 fixes this by widening the label to include *what* stopped the rollout,
not just when.

## Supporting data

[`v1_ablation_report.md`](v1_ablation_report.md) — the full ablation study
(drop-space, unsorted, averaged-concatenation, pool-sensitivity, linear
baseline, and a noise-level sweep) run under this version's architecture.
The qualitative findings (noise dominates prediction quality; MLP clearly
beats the linear baseline; concatenation beats averaging; every space
contributes) all still hold under v2 — only the label scheme changed, not
the underlying signal — but the exact numbers in that report are not
comparable to v2's (a 5-way and a 13-way classification problem don't share
a loss/accuracy scale).
